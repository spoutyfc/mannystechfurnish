# AEC Market Research — Manny's Tech Furnish

Prepared 2026-10-27 for the "AEC Digital Growth Partner" offer ($1,000/month, 6-month initial term).
Scope: small and midsize Bay Area engineering and construction firms (structural, civil, geotechnical,
MEP, surveying, general engineering contractors, design-build).

**Label key.** Every statement carries one label:
- **[Fact — S#]** sourced; S# refers to the Sources table at the bottom.
- **[Assumption]** reasoned but not verified; needs confirmation from conversations with principals.
- **[Recommendation]** what Manny should do.

**Evidence quality note.** Most current AEC buyer research is paywalled (Zweig full report, Hinge
"Inside the Buyer's Brain", SMPS Foundation/FMI). Only the public summaries were read. Several referral
statistics are old (2006, 2012); they are labeled with their dates and should not be quoted as current
on the website. **[Fact — S1, S5, S6, S7, S9]**

## 1. How small and midsize AEC firms buy and sell professional services

### 1.1 Business development is principal-led
- 55% of AEC firms rely primarily on seller-doers for business development; 45% employ dedicated BD
  staff. **[Fact — S1, Zweig Group 2025 Marketing & BD Report, published 2025-09-24]**
- 79% of surveyed firms have a full-time marketing leader and nearly 60% combine marketing and BD in
  one function. **[Fact — S1]** The Zweig sample skews toward firms large enough to answer a marketing
  survey. **[Assumption]**
- Median firm-wide marketing budget was $400,000; large firms exceed $3 million. **[Fact — S1]**
- Small Bay Area firms (roughly 3–25 staff) usually have no marketing coordinator at all; the principal
  PE/SE or owner-contractor does BD between billable work. **[Assumption — inferred from the
  seller-doer finding plus firm size; confirm in discovery calls]**
- 33% of AEC firms use AI regularly in marketing/BD and 39% are testing it. **[Fact — S1]**
- 47% of firms cite poor adoption of BD tools as a challenge (up from 35% in 2024); 20% have no CRM at
  all; only 40% consistently use a formal go/no-go process. **[Fact — S2, Unanet 2025 AEC Inspire
  Report, 300+ leaders]**

Implication: the buyer is a time-poor technical principal, not a marketing department. The offer must
take work off their desk, not add a dashboard to it. **[Recommendation]**

### 1.2 Referrals and repeat clients dominate new work
- In an AIM study of facility directors, construction managers and end users, 64% of decisions were
  based on peer recommendations, 33% on contractor recommendations, 21% networking, 14% web searches.
  **[Fact — S5, published 2012-12-17; dated]**
- Zweig Group wrote that AEC firms "usually" have around an 80% repeat-client rate and that this is
  merely average. **[Fact — S6, published 2006-05-15; dated]**
- Hinge's 4th-edition AEC buyer study (~300 buyers, 1,400+ sellers) reports that asking peers for
  recommendations and web searches remain the primary ways buyers look for information, LinkedIn use
  for business is at an all-time high, and client service entered the top five selection drivers.
  **[Fact — S7, published 2022-12-15]**
- The SMPS Foundation/FMI study (200+ professionals) describes the industry moving from
  relationship/proximity selling toward a more research-informed approach to marketing and client
  engagement, with client experience as a strategic imperative. **[Fact — S8]**

Implication: a website for an engineering firm rarely *creates* the first contact; it *confirms* a
referral, a GC's recommendation or an agency shortlist. Its job is to pass the "verify" step quickly and
make the firm look like it is in the right league. **[Assumption — consistent with S5, S7]**
Manny's sales copy must never promise that the website replaces referrals. **[Recommendation]**

### 1.3 Public work is qualifications-based, not price-based
- California Government Code §§4525–4529.5 (the state "Mini-Brooks Act") requires public entities to
  select architectural, engineering, land surveying, environmental and construction project management
  firms on demonstrated competence and qualifications, with fees negotiated afterward, rather than by
  competitive bidding. **[Fact — S10]**
- ACEC Research Institute analysis found projects procured through QBS had lower cost growth (3% vs
  6% national average), lower schedule growth (7% vs 10%), and 89% high or very high owner
  satisfaction. **[Fact — S11, S12, published 2022-03-09]**
- Public agencies therefore issue RFQs and evaluate SOQs (statements of qualifications) built from
  project sheets, resumes, licenses, references and similar-project experience. **[Fact — S10 describes
  the qualifications-based procedure; the document names are standard industry usage — Assumption for
  the exact naming each Bay Area agency uses]**

Implication: the same assets that win QBS pursuits — project sheets with scope, client, role, size,
dates, and licensed staff — are what the website should publish. One "growth asset per month" should
double as SOQ-ready content. **[Recommendation]**

### 1.4 Licensing is a trust signal buyers can check
- California's BPELSG offers a public license lookup for PEs, land surveyors, geologists and
  geophysicists. **[Fact — S13]**
- "Structural Engineer" and "Geotechnical Engineer" are title authorities granted to licensed civil
  engineers; only the title use is restricted, and only an SE may design hospitals and public schools.
  **[Fact — S14, S15]**

Implication: the site should show license numbers and title authorities accurately per person, link to
the state lookup, and Manny must never write "structural engineer" or "geotechnical engineer" for anyone
whose license has not been verified. A copy-review check against the BPELSG lookup should be part of
the build checklist. **[Recommendation]**

### 1.5 Local search mechanics (Google Business Profile)
- Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence; complete,
  detailed business information helps relevance; more reviews and positive ratings can help prominence;
  and there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. **[Fact — S3]**
- Service-area businesses that do not serve customers at their address must hide the address; up to
  20 service areas may be listed, generally within about 2 hours' drive. **[Fact — S4]**

Implication: Manny can honestly offer GBP *guidance* (complete categories, services, service areas,
project photos, review-request process) but cannot sell rankings. This matches the offer's exclusion
of guaranteed rankings. Distance is outside anyone's control. **[Recommendation]**

## 3. What AEC buyers look for on an engineering firm website

| Buyer | What they need to confirm | Label |
|-------|---------------------------|-------|
| All buyers | Evidence of relevant past projects at the right scale, for comparable clients | [Fact — S16, S20] |
| All buyers | Who the team is and whether they have deep (not surface) expertise; process and structure | [Fact — S16, S20] |
| All buyers | References/testimonials with project names and clients | [Fact — S16] |
| All buyers | Certifications and quality practices | [Fact — S16] |
| All buyers | Only claims the firm can deliver ("guarantee what you can absolutely do") | [Fact — S16] |
| All buyers | Clarity and rigor; buyers compare sites side by side | [Fact — S20, agency claim] |
| Homeowners | Do they handle my problem (foundation crack, ADU, retrofit, plan-check corrections), in my city, and how do I start? | [Assumption] |
| Developers / owners | Comparable project types, schedule reliability, licensed principals, responsiveness | [Assumption] |
| Architects | Discipline coverage, coordination ability, turnaround, ease of teaming | [Assumption] |
| Public agencies | Similar public projects, licenses, certifications (DBE/SBE), staff resumes, references, local presence | [Fact — S10 for qualifications basis; specifics Assumption] |

Recommendations for every client site:
1. A filterable project portfolio (type, discipline, city, client type) with a project-sheet template:
   location, client type, role, scope, size/value range where the client permits, year, team. **[Recommendation]**
2. Team page with verified license numbers and BPELSG/CSLB lookup links. **[Recommendation]**
3. Service pages written in buyer vocabulary and mapped to GBP services and service areas. **[Recommendation]**
4. Separate entry paths: "Homeowner", "Architect / developer", "Public agency / prime". **[Recommendation]**
5. A fee-proposal / project-intake form asking project type, city, timeline and documents available.
   **[Recommendation]**
6. No invented testimonials, logos or metrics; only client-approved project data. **[Recommendation]**

## 5. Implications for Manny's site copy and offer

1. **Positioning:** "Website and local growth partner for Bay Area engineering and construction
   firms" is defensible: specialist agencies are national and expensive; local shops are generalist.
   **[Recommendation]**
2. **Headline logic:** speak to the verification moment ("When a referral looks you up, your site
   should make the shortlist decision easy") rather than lead generation. **[Recommendation]**
3. **Proof Manny can honestly show today:** process, deliverable examples, a sample project-sheet
   template, published price and scope. Do not show client logos, metrics or testimonials until real
   ones exist. **[Recommendation; required by studio quality rules]**
4. **Growth asset menu (one per month):** project sheet / case study, service-area page, service page
   in buyer vocabulary, team/credentials page update, GBP post and photo set guidance, SOQ-ready PDF
   export of a project. **[Recommendation]**
5. **Language rules:** use the Section 2 vocabulary; never claim rankings, leads or revenue (S3 says
   ranking cannot be bought); never assign license titles without verification (S14). **[Recommendation]**
6. **Price presentation:** publish $1,000/month and the 6-month total ($6,000) with the build included.
   Compared with published ranges (freelancer premium site $7,000–$15,000 plus maintenance; specialist
   AEC agency $25,000–$60,000 — S26), it reads as accessible. Risk: it may read as "too cheap to be
   specialist"; counter with tight, specific scope. **[Recommendation]**
7. **Sales motion:** principals buy on referral; Manny should sell through referrals too (architects,
   GCs, existing clients) and treat his own site as the verification step. **[Recommendation]**
8. **No conversion rates** are assumed or quoted anywhere in this research. **[Fact — none found in
   primary sources for this niche]**
